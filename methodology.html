Sources, coverage & screening method

Snapshot: September 6, 2026. Listing status can change at any time. This is a discovery catalogue, not an appraisal, safety assessment or verified shortlist.

500 active candidates, not 500 fully qualified matches. No candidate’s land-lease status has been verified. Current school quality, total carrying costs, physical condition and door-to-door commute still require checking. Unknown values are not zero.

What the expanded search found

We screened 3,569 distinct source properties from the first results page of 132 ZIP-code searches on OneKey MLS. After preliminary screening, 817 were active, priced at $1.2 million or less, had at least two bedrooms, and were condos or single-family/attached single-family homes. Individual property pages were then checked until 500 active homes were confirmed: 151 condos, 269 single-family houses and 80 townhouses/attached homes. Three candidates had changed to pending or hold and were excluded from the active selection. Three others were excluded because their addresses were undisclosed or conflicted with the geocoded location.

The search covers Manhattan, Queens, Brooklyn and western Nassau through the Hicksville area (eastern longitude cutoff −73.49). It does not cover the Bronx, Staten Island or Suffolk. Each ZIP’s first page contains up to 30 results: this is neither an exhaustive MLS feed nor a random sample. All eligible discovered condos were prioritized, then remaining houses by straight-line proximity to MTA rail. This favors near-rail houses and does not guarantee a convenient actual commute. Co-ops and multifamily listings were excluded from new candidates.

All original 45 IDs remain available so saved likes and notes survive. Thirteen originals were refreshed (12 active, one coming soon); 32 could not be reverified and retain clearly labeled provisional prior facts. Combined with new candidates, the catalogue contains 533 records. Older retained records need not satisfy the new screening criteria.

Audit downloads: refresh summary, ZIP search URLs and retrieval times, 3,569 screened property records (CSV). These contain public listing facts, never household likes or notes.

Listing facts, costs, schools and commute

  • Every refreshed card links to its individual source listing and names the listing brokerage and MLS identifier where supplied. Price, availability, bed/bath counts, area, build year, tax year and school names are source-reported, not independently inspected. Attached single-family records are grouped as townhouses/attached; condominium ownership remains in Condos.
  • Annual property taxes are divided by 12. HOA/common charges use explicitly supplied fees and frequency. A condo without a reported fee remains unknown even if its association flag is false. Taxes and fees exclude mortgage, insurance, utilities, assessments and closing costs. The carrying-cost sort is HOA plus tax only, with incomplete totals last. Low fees are a preference, not a hard exclusion; missing fees need follow-up.
  • Among 500 active homes, 499 have a build year, 493 a tax amount, 452 a known HOA/common-charge amount, and 463 at least one named school (467 have a school or district name; “Contact Agent” placeholders are excluded). Schools are listing-agent assignments, not verified enrollment eligibility or quality ratings. The preserved Best schools filter only uses original 4/5+ screening estimates, explicitly unverified. New unrated homes are not presumed worse. Verify directly with the school district.
  • Build year and condition are separate. A newer home is not automatically in better condition. Condition is source-reported where available; otherwise it requires inspection. Original fit scores and rail-minute estimates are retained only as unverified prior estimates; no new ratings or journey times were invented.
  • Nearest rail is measured from MLS address coordinates to official MTA subway stations and MTA railroad stations (LIRR), retrieved September 6. Distances are straight-line, not walking routes or station entrances. Across the 501 refreshed records, 497 locations are address points and four are street-interpolated. Check transfers, schedules, entrances and first/last-mile travel.

Bikeability — proximity is only one indicator

NYC: distance to the nearest current Class I (protected/off-street) segment in the NYC DOT Bicycle Routes dataset, network snapshot July 24, 2026. This covers all 359 active NYC homes. Distance is to the line segment, not necessarily an accessible entrance. A nearby segment may be disconnected from your desired route.

Nassau: distance to a mapped separated cycleway/track or bicycle-designated path in © OpenStreetMap contributors, snapshot September 6, 2026. Each card links to the closest mapped way. We queried the bounding box 40.57, −73.80, 40.88, −73.49 for highway=cycleway, cycleway/left/right=track, or highway=path with bicycle=designated; excluded explicit private/no access and no/private/dismount bicycle tags. Mapping may omit facilities, and a path may be unpaved. An unmarked restriction or unavailable entrance can change access. This is a different source and classification from NYC, not a comparable network-quality score.

The quarter-mile filter and bike-distance sort use straight-line proximity to these mapped facilities. They do not establish a safe, connected or comfortable trip. Check pavement, hills, traffic stress, crossings, route continuity, storage, lighting and actual access in person. Derived Nassau proximity data is attributed to OpenStreetMap and available under the Open Database License (ODbL 1.0); this does not relicense independent MLS or government data.

Likes and learning

Private shared likes, reason tags and notes still sync between your devices. The new Bikeability and Crime safety tags let you explicitly state why you like a home. The taste profile finds common attributes and explains similarity recommendations; these are observable patterns, not a trained model or certainty about your preferences. Bike proximity can influence recommendations when you explicitly choose Bikeability. Crime counts never become a safety score. New recommendations exclude records marked inactive or unverified.
